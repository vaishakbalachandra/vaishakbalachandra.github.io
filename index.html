Research

A price is a noisy observation of something underneath it: what a company is worth, what a crowd believes, what a few people already know. A Kalman filter can estimate a hidden state like that, but only because it knows the rules by which the state moves. Markets do not offer that comfort. Their rules change, usually without announcing it, and a model that worked yesterday can be quietly wrong today.

My research interest has two parts.

Estimation and detection

Prices are a fast channel; company filings and ownership disclosures are slow, sparse and partly written in words. Can a model that reads both detect a change in a company's regime before the price reflects it, and what, measurably, separates the two channels when they disagree?

Decision

Once such an estimate exists, with an error attached and some probability that the regime has already moved, how should capital be allocated on it when the model itself may be wrong?

Keywords latent-state estimation; regime switching; mixed-frequency data; optimal stopping; robust optimization; partial observability

Projects

Privacy by architecture for retrieval-augmented generation

M.S. thesis, 2025–2026

Problem

Retrieval systems for language models encode every sensitive attribute of a record together and add protection afterwards, so information can leak across attributes.

Idea

Split each record by attribute before encoding, so that no single component ever observes a whole record, and recombine only at the end.

Result

Of four systems tested on synthetic medical records, three gradually exhausted their privacy budget; the multi-model design kept all of it, with faithfulness comparable to the baseline.

Built with Python, PyTorch, vLLM, LangGraph and a vector database. Read the thesis
